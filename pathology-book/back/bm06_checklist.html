Pathology: All-in-One · Rapid revision

Last-Day Checklist

Forty facts you must be able to say aloud the evening before the paper. Tick each one only if you can explain it without looking.

GIT & liver

Barrett oesophagus = columnar mucosa with goblet cells; precursor of adenocarcinoma, not SCC.1

H. pylori: antral gastritis → duodenal ulcer, gastric carcinoma, MALT lymphoma. Autoimmune gastritis → B12 deficiency.2

Diffuse gastric cancer: signet-ring cells, linitis plastica, CDH1; ovarian spread = Krukenberg.2

Crohn: skip lesions, transmural, granulomas, fistulae. UC: from rectum, continuous, mucosal, pseudopolyps.3

Adenoma–carcinoma: APC → KRAS → TP53; large, villous, high-grade adenomas carry most risk.4

HBV: anti-HBc IgM = acute; HBsAg beyond 6 months = chronic; anti-HBs alone = vaccinated.5

Cirrhosis = diffuse fibrosis + regenerative nodules (stellate cells); HCC: HCV in Pakistan, AFP.6

Haemochromatosis: HFE C282Y, low hepcidin, Perls-positive liver; bronze diabetes.6

Acute pancreatitis: gallstones, alcohol; lipase; enzymatic fat necrosis; pseudocyst.7

Lung

Lobar pneumonia: congestion → red → grey hepatisation → resolution; S. pneumoniae.8

TB granuloma: caseation, epithelioid cells, Langhans cells; type IV hypersensitivity; ZN stain.8

Emphysema: centriacinar (smokers, upper lobes) vs panacinar (α1-AT, lower lobes).9

Lung cancer: SCC → PTHrP; small cell → ACTH, ADH, TP53 + RB1; adenocarcinoma → EGFR, ALK.10

Blood

IDA: ferritin low, TIBC high. Chronic disease: ferritin high, TIBC low, hepcidin up.11

B12 deficiency = megaloblastic anaemia + nerve damage; folate spares the nerves.11

β-thal major: HbF high, HbA absent or low, target cells, crew-cut skull, iron overload.12

AML ≥ 20% blasts, MPO+, Auer rods; APL t(15;17) → DIC, ATRA. ALL: TdT+, children.13

CML: t(9;22) BCR–ABL, basophilia, low LAP, big spleen; imatinib.13

Translocations: 8;14 Burkitt · 14;18 follicular · 11;14 mantle cell · 11;22 Ewing.14

Reed–Sternberg: CD15+ CD30+, owl-eye nucleoli; popcorn cells (NLPHL) CD20+.14

DIC: low platelets and fibrinogen, long PT and aPTT, high D-dimer, schistocytes.15

Kidney

Nephritic: haematuria, RBC casts, hypertension. Nephrotic: protein > 3.5 g/day, albumin < 3 g/dL, lipids.16

PSGN: subepithelial humps, low C3, ASO up. MCD: normal LM, foot-process effacement.16

Clear cell RCC: VHL (3p), golden-yellow, renal vein spread, polycythaemia.18

Reproductive & breast

Seminoma: clear cells, lymphocytic septa, AFP normal. Yolk sac: AFP, Schiller–Duval bodies.19

Prostate cancer: peripheral zone, no basal cells, Gleason (architecture), osteoblastic metastases.19

HPV 16/18: E6 → p53, E7 → RB; CIN 1 = LSIL, CIN 2–3 = HSIL.20

Moles: complete 46,XX paternal, no fetus; partial 69,XXY with fetal parts.21

Breast: IDC commonest; ILC loses E-cadherin; nodal status = strongest prognostic factor.22

Endocrine

PTC: clear grooved nuclei, psammoma, node spread. Follicular: capsular/vascular invasion, blood spread.23

MTC: C cells, calcitonin, amyloid, RET. Hashimoto: lymphoid follicles, Hürthle cells, anti-TPO.23

Diabetes: type 1 insulitis vs type 2 islet amyloid; KW nodules; hyaline arteriolosclerosis.24

Heart & vessels

Atheroma: fatty streak → fibrofatty plaque; thin-cap rupture → thrombosis → MI.25

MI: neutrophils days 1–3; macrophages days 3–7 (rupture risk); granulation from day 7; scar by 2 months.26

Rheumatic heart: Aschoff bodies, Anitschkow cells; mitral stenosis with fish-mouth orifice.26

Bone, joints, CNS, skin

Bone tumour sites: epiphysis GCT; metaphysis osteosarcoma, osteochondroma; diaphysis Ewing (t(11;22)).27

Gout: negatively birefringent needles. Pseudogout: positive rhomboids. RA: pannus, anti-CCP.28

CSF: bacterial = neutrophils, low glucose; viral = lymphocytes, normal glucose; TB = lymphocytes, very high protein.29

Brain tumours: GBM pseudopalisading; pilocytic Rosenthal fibres; meningioma psammoma; schwannoma Verocay.30

Skin: pemphigus suprabasal, Dsg3; pemphigoid subepidermal; melanoma prognosis = Breslow thickness.31

  • First 5 minutes: read the whole SEQ paper and start with the question you know best.
  • Budget time: one minute per BCQ; 12–13 minutes per 5-mark SEQ. Never leave a part blank.
  • BCQs: read the last line first, underline age, sex and site, eliminate, and change an answer only for a clear reason.
  • SEQs: definition → flowchart → gross then microscopy → complications; use a table for every “compare”.
  • Slides: organ → process → benign or malignant → name + two features; know the stain or marker.
  • Viva: open with the definition, classify, then steer to what you know well; say so if you do not know.
