MARKING Part 2 · Marking scheme
SEQ 1Marking scheme · 5 marksH. pylori and peptic ulcer
  1. 1Helicobacter pylori (0.5); any two of urease, flagella, adhesins, CagA, VacA (0.25 each).
  2. 2Antral-predominant gastritis (0.5) → less somatostatin, more gastrin → acid hypersecretion (0.5) → gastric metaplasia in the duodenum, which the organism colonises (0.5) → inflammation, ammonia and cytokines weaken mucosal defence, so acid and pepsin ulcerate it (0.5).
  3. 2Gross: round to oval, sharply punched-out, usually < 2 cm, straight walls, flat margins, clean base, radiating folds (1). Microscopy: necrotic fibrinoid debris, neutrophilic layer, granulation tissue, fibrous scar (0.5). Complications: bleeding, perforation, pyloric obstruction (0.5).
SEQ 2Marking scheme · 5 marksCirrhosis and portal hypertension
  1. 1A diffuse process (0.25) with bridging fibrous septa (0.25) and parenchymal regenerative nodules (0.25) that destroys normal lobular architecture (0.25).
  2. 2Chronic injury (HCV) → Kupffer cells and lymphocytes release TGF-β, PDGF, TNF (0.5) → stellate cells activate into myofibroblasts (0.5) → collagen I and III in the space of Disse; sinusoids lose fenestrae (capillarisation) (0.5) → septa link portal tracts and central veins; surviving hepatocytes form nodules (0.5).
  3. 2Any four, 0.5 each: ascites; portosystemic shunts (oesophageal varices causing haematemesis, rectal varices, caput medusae); congestive splenomegaly with hypersplenism; hepatic encephalopathy.
SEQ 3Marking scheme · 5 marksPulmonary tuberculosis
  1. 1Secondary (post-primary) pulmonary tuberculosis (0.5); sputum ZN smear for AFB, GeneXpert MTB/RIF, culture (any two, 0.25 each).
  2. 2Bacilli are taken up by alveolar macrophages and survive by blocking phagolysosome fusion (0.5) → after about 3 weeks antigens are presented and Th1 cells develop under IL-12 (0.5) → IFN-γ activates macrophages into epithelioid cells and Langhans giant cells (0.5) → TNF recruits monocytes; caseous necrosis reflects type IV hypersensitivity (0.5).
  3. 2Four rows, 0.5 each: unsensitised vs sensitised host; subpleural mid-lung vs apical site; Ghon complex vs cavitating fibrocaseous lesion; prominent vs minimal nodes; healing and calcification vs progression.
SEQ 4Marking scheme · 5 marksβ-thalassaemia major
  1. 1β-thalassaemia major (homozygous β-thalassaemia).
  2. 1Haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC (0.5): HbF markedly raised, HbA absent or very low (0.5).
  3. 2β-globin mutations reduce or abolish β-chains (0.5) → less HbA, so cells are microcytic and hypochromic (0.5) → unpaired α-chains precipitate, damaging precursors in the marrow (ineffective erythropoiesis) and red cells in the spleen (haemolysis) (0.5) → anaemia drives erythropoietin, marrow expansion (frontal bossing, crew-cut skull) and extramedullary haematopoiesis (0.5).
  4. 1Repeated transfusions (0.5) and increased gut absorption because ineffective erythropoiesis suppresses hepcidin (0.5).
SEQ 5Marking scheme · 5 marksPost-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
  1. 1Acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (0.5); raised ASO or anti-DNase B and low C3 (0.5).
  2. 2Nephritogenic group A streptococci → latent period of 1–4 weeks → immune complexes (e.g. SpeB) in glomeruli → complement activation → neutrophil and monocyte influx (1). LM: enlarged hypercellular glomeruli with neutrophils; IF: granular IgG and C3; EM: subepithelial humps (1).
  3. 2Any four, 0.5 each: haematuria with RBC casts vs none; proteinuria < 3.5 vs > 3.5 g/day; periorbital vs generalised oedema; hypertension vs normal BP; hyperlipidaemia and lipiduria only in nephrotic.
SEQ 6Marking scheme · 5 marksInvasive breast carcinoma
  1. 1Any four, 0.25 each: age; early menarche or late menopause; nulliparity or late first pregnancy; family history or BRCA1/2; postmenopausal obesity; hormone therapy; atypical hyperplasia; chest radiation.
  2. 2Gross: hard grey-white stellate mass with infiltrative edges, gritty on cutting, chalky streaks; skin or nipple retraction (1). Microscopy: cords, nests and glands of pleomorphic cells with mitoses in a desmoplastic stroma, invading fat, no myoepithelial layer; graded on tubules, pleomorphism and mitoses (1).
  3. 2Prognostic: nodal status (most important), size, distant metastasis, grade, lymphovascular invasion, histological type (1). Predictive: ER and PR for hormone therapy, HER2 for trastuzumab; Ki-67 as proliferation index (1).
SEQ 7Marking scheme · 5 marksPapillary carcinoma of the thyroid
  1. 1Papillary thyroid carcinoma (0.5); ionising radiation in childhood and family history, with BRAF or RET/PTC as the molecular basis (0.5).
  2. 2Gross: solitary or multifocal, grey-white, may be cystic or calcified (0.5). Microscopy: branching papillae with fibrovascular cores (0.5); optically clear Orphan Annie-eye nuclei, grooves and pseudoinclusions (0.5); psammoma bodies and lymphatic invasion (0.5).
  3. 2Four rows, 0.5 each: diagnosis by nuclear features vs by capsular or vascular invasion; spread to nodes vs blood (lung, bone); BRAF, RET/PTC vs RAS, PAX8–PPARG; psammoma bodies present vs absent.
SEQ 8Marking scheme · 5 marksAtherosclerosis and myocardial infarction
  1. 2Endothelial dysfunction from hypertension, smoking, hyperlipidaemia, diabetes (0.5) → LDL enters the intima and is oxidised; monocytes adhere (VCAM-1) and become macrophages that take up oxidised LDL → foam cells, fatty streak (0.5) → cytokines and PDGF draw in and multiply smooth muscle cells that make matrix (0.5) → fibrous cap over a lipid–necrotic core: the fibrofatty plaque; rupture of a thin cap triggers thrombosis (0.5).
  2. 1Cardiac troponin I or T (0.5): most specific and sensitive for myocardial necrosis, rises within hours and stays raised for 1–2 weeks (0.5).
  3. 2Any four with timing, 0.5 each: arrhythmias (first hours to days); cardiogenic shock or pump failure (early); pericarditis (2–3 days; Dressler weeks later); rupture of free wall, septum or papillary muscle (3–7 days); mural thrombus with embolism; ventricular aneurysm (weeks to months).
SEQ 9Marking scheme · 5 marksOsteosarcoma
  1. 1Osteosarcoma (0.5); any two of hereditary retinoblastoma (RB1), Li–Fraumeni (TP53), Paget disease, prior radiation, bone infarcts (0.5).
  2. 2Gross: large grey-white gritty tumour with haemorrhage and cystic change, destroying cortex, lifting periosteum into soft tissue, usually stopping at the growth plate (1). Microscopy: pleomorphic malignant cells with atypical mitoses and giant cells that lay down lace-like neoplastic osteoid directly; cartilage may be present (1).
  3. 2Four rows, 0.5 each: age (osteosarcoma and Ewing mostly under 20; GCT 20–40 years); site (metaphysis vs diaphysis vs epiphysis); X-ray (sunburst, Codman vs onion-skin vs soap-bubble); microscopy (malignant osteoid vs small round blue CD99+ cells vs osteoclast-like giant cells).
