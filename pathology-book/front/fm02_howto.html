How to use this book

Every chapter is built the same way, so you always know where to look, what to learn and how to test yourself.

Anatomy of a chapter

Each of the 31 chapters covers the lectures, interactive sessions and labs of one block of the syllabus (see the Syllabus Map). Six parts appear in the same order every time.

The 3-pass method

Reading a chapter once feels like learning; recalling it with the book shut is what actually makes it stay. Take every chapter through three passes.

1
Learn

In the week of the lecture (the Syllabus Map tells you which chapter). Read the concept sections, think through every Concept Clear box and copy each flowchart once by hand.

Tracker: tick “Read”
2
Compress

Within two days. Squeeze each disease into the six headings on one side of paper, fill the comparison tables from memory and add your own notes to the Night-before page.

Tracker: tick “Compressed”
3
Recall

Book closed. Do the BCQs, answer the viva aloud, fill the gaps and draw the two figures from memory. Then mark, and reread only what you got wrong.

Tracker: tick “BCQs done”

The boxes: a legend

Ten kinds of box recur throughout the book. Each has one job; once you know the job, you know how to use the box.

The wording that opens an SEQ answer and earns the definition mark.

Barrett oesophagus: intestinal metaplasia (columnar epithelium with goblet cells) replacing the squamous lining of the distal oesophagus, as a complication of long-standing GERD.

The why behind a fact, so you can reason instead of memorising.

Antral H. pylori gastritis lowers somatostatin, so gastrin and acid output rise and the duodenum ulcerates. Autoimmune gastritis destroys parietal cells instead: acid falls and the risk becomes B12 deficiency, not ulcer.

Local burden, risk factors and practice that examiners in Pakistan expect.

Smokeless tobacco (naswar, gutka) and betel quid with areca nut make oral cancer one of the commonest cancers in Pakistani men. Areca nut also causes oral submucous fibrosis.

An accurate mnemonic worth carrying into the exam hall.

CRAB

Multiple myeloma: hyperCalcaemia, Renal failure, Anaemia, Bone (lytic) lesions.

Look-alikes, classic BCQ traps, and the Robbins version where teachers differ.

A raised AFP rules out a pure seminoma. Seminoma may secrete some hCG (from syncytiotrophoblastic cells), but a raised AFP means a non-seminomatous (yolk sac) component.

Reference ranges, cut-offs and how to read a test.

Microcytic MCV < 80 fL  ·  Macrocytic MCV > 100 fL

Iron deficiency ferritin ↓, TIBC ↑, saturation ↓

Chronic disease ferritin normal or ↑, TIBC ↓

Vignette → reasoning → diagnosis: how BCQ stems and OSPE scenarios are written.

A 58-year-old smoker has haematuria, a loin mass and haemoglobin 18.5 g/dL. Reasoning: renal mass plus secondary polycythaemia (tumour erythropoietin). Diagnosis: renal cell carcinoma, clear cell type.

Links the lesion to the bedside: signs, tests, complications.

Why does a patient with cirrhosis bleed? Less synthesis of factors II, VII, IX and X (prolonged PT), low platelets from hypersplenism, and varices from portal hypertension.

The one gene, translocation or marker examiners ask, and what it does.

CML: t(9;22) Philadelphia chromosome → BCR–ABL1 fusion gene → constitutively active tyrosine kinase → the target of imatinib.

How to name the slide or specimen in ten seconds.

Papillary carcinoma thyroid: branching papillae with fibrovascular cores; crowded, clear “Orphan Annie eye” nuclei with grooves and inclusions; psammoma bodies.

Morphology panel · example: Hashimoto thyroiditis
  • Thyroid diffusely, symmetrically enlarged; capsule intact
  • Cut surface pale grey-tan, firm, somewhat friable
  • Gland well demarcated from neighbouring structures
  • Dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with germinal centres
  • Atrophic follicles; Hürthle cells (abundant pink granular cytoplasm)
  • Increased interstitial fibrous tissue
How to read a panel: gross = size, shape, colour, consistency, cut surface; microscopy = architecture → cells → special features; the bottom strip adds the clinical link (here: painless goitre, slow hypothyroidism, anti-TPO antibodies, risk of B-cell lymphoma).
