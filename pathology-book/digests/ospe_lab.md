# Special Pathology labs & OSPE — what students see (BUMDC lab list + Module 12 OSPE notes)

## Lab slides/specimens listed in the syllabus
- Module 10 labs: H. pylori gastritis · Chronic pancreatitis · Chronic hepatitis · Cirrhosis · Hepatocellular carcinoma · Pneumonia · Pulmonary TB · Squamous cell carcinoma lung · Gross specimens Mod X · Anaemias (peripheral smears) · Leukaemias (peripheral smears/marrow)
- Module 11 labs: Renal cell carcinoma · Transitional cell carcinoma · Seminoma · BPH · Adenomyosis and endometrial carcinoma · Mucinous cystadenoma ovary and trophoblastic diseases · Fibroadenoma breast · Breast carcinoma · Hashimoto's thyroiditis · Papillary carcinoma thyroid · Gross specimens
- Module 12 labs: Osteochondroma · Osteosarcoma · Giant cell tumour of bone · Malignant melanoma · Schwannoma · Brain tumours · Bone tumours

## Module 12 OSPE notes used by students (BUMDC "Special Pathology Mod 3 OSPE") — stations and the points they test
- Osteochondroma: exostosis; commonest benign bone tumour; ~85% solitary, rest multiple hereditary exostoses (EXT1/EXT2); men 3×; metaphysis near growth plate, knee; cartilage cap of hyaline cartilage with perichondrium, enchondral ossification, cortex and medulla continuous with host bone.
- Osteosarcoma: malignant cells produce osteoid; commonest primary malignant bone tumour; bimodal (75% < 20 y; second peak elderly with Paget, bone infarcts, radiation); metaphysis of long bones, ~50% around knee; mixed lytic–blastic destructive mass; RB, TP53, INK4a, MDM2/CDK4 amplification; gross gritty grey-white with haemorrhage/cystic change, destroys cortex; lung metastasis (10–20% at diagnosis); 5-yr survival 60–70% without metastasis.
- Ewing sarcoma: primitive small round cells; 2nd commonest bone sarcoma in children; diaphysis of long bones, pelvis; onion-skin periosteal reaction; t(11;22)(q24;q12) EWS–FLI1; Homer-Wright rosettes; glycogen-rich clear cytoplasm; 5-yr survival ~75% with chemo; chemo-induced necrosis is prognostic.
- Chondrosarcoma: ≥ 40 years; axial skeleton (pelvis, shoulder, ribs); conventional (central/peripheral), clear cell, dedifferentiated, mesenchymal; grey-white translucent glistening nodules, myxoid; grade drives prognosis.
- Osteomyelitis: S. aureus commonest; E. coli/Pseudomonas/Klebsiella (UTI, IV drugs); neonates H. influenzae/GBS; sickle cell Salmonella; TB (Pott disease); syphilis (sabre shin); lytic focus with sclerosis; complications: fracture, amyloidosis, endocarditis, sepsis, (SCC in sinus tract).
- Giant cell tumour (osteoclastoma): 20–40 y; benign but locally aggressive; RANKL from neoplastic osteoblast precursors drives osteoclast-type giant cells; epiphysis (only epiphyseal tumour of this group), knee; red-brown, cystic, thin shell of reactive bone; uniform mononuclear cells with giant cells having up to 100 nuclei.
- AML: ≥ 20% myeloid blasts; Auer rods (numerous in t(15;17)); MPO+; t(8;21), inv(16); monoblasts NSE+; aleukaemic leukaemia.
- CML: t(9;22) BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase; hypercellular marrow, increased eosinophils/basophils, sea-blue histiocytes; WBC often > 100,000/mm³ with full spectrum of maturing granulocytes.
- Schwannoma: NF2/merlin; encapsulated; Antoni A (dense, palisading, Verocay bodies) and Antoni B (loose, myxoid); acoustic neuroma (vestibular CN VIII) — tinnitus, hearing loss.
- Medulloblastoma: children, cerebellar midline; radiosensitive; Homer-Wright rosettes; WNT (best), SHH, group 3 (MYC, worst), group 4.
- Meningioma: dura-attached, arachnoid cap cells; prior radiation; NF2, chr 22 loss; WHO grades 1–3; whorls, psammoma bodies.
- Ependymoma: near ventricles/central canal; children (4th ventricle), adults spinal cord (NF2); perivascular pseudorosettes, true ependymal rosettes; GFAP+.
- Pilocytic (cystic) astrocytoma: children, cerebellum; cyst with mural nodule; Rosenthal fibres, eosinophilic granular bodies; BRAF alterations; grade 1.
- Glioblastoma: supratentorial, crosses corpus callosum ("butterfly"); variegated cut surface (necrosis, haemorrhage); pseudopalisading necrosis, microvascular proliferation; grade 4; median survival ~15 months.
- Cerebral infarct: MCA territory; 12 h red neurons; 24–48 h neutrophils; then macrophages; reactive astrocytes ~1 week; months later gliosis/cavity; vulnerable neurons: hippocampal CA1 (Sommer sector), Purkinje cells, cortical pyramidal neurons; BE-FAST.
- Malignant melanoma: UV, fair skin, xeroderma pigmentosum, dysplastic naevus syndrome; CDKN2A, BRAF, NRAS; nests of pleomorphic cells with melanin; prognostic: Breslow thickness, mitoses, ulceration, TILs; ABCDE.
- Atherosclerosis: risk factors; response-to-injury pathogenesis; fatty streak → fibrofatty plaque; complications.
- Myocardial infarction: troponins rise 3–12 h, CK-MB and cTnI peak ~24 h; CK-MB normal by 48–72 h; cTnI 5–10 days, cTnT 5–14 days; complications.

## How BUMDC/UHS OSPE stations are usually set (pattern)
- "Identify the lesion" on a projected or microscope slide + 2–3 features + one question on aetiology/complication/marker.
- Gross specimen jar: identify organ, lesion, two gross features, one complication.
- Peripheral smear (anaemia, leukaemia) with CBC values: interpret.
- Data stations: LFTs, RFTs/ABG, urinalysis, CSF, coagulation profile, thyroid profile, semen? (not in path), hepatitis serology.
