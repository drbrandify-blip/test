# Pakistan Focus facts sheet (use these consistently; never invent a precise figure)

## Infections
- Hepatitis: PMRC national survey 2007–08: anti-HCV ≈ 4.8%, HBsAg ≈ 2.5% of the general population (exam-standard figures). Pakistan carries one of the largest hepatitis C burdens in the world (WHO reports). Unsafe injections, reuse of syringes, unscreened blood, barbers' razors and dental procedures are the main routes. HDV co-infection is relatively frequent in HBV carriers in some regions (write "relatively common", no number).
- HCV is the leading cause of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma in Pakistan (write as "leading cause"; HBV second).
- Tuberculosis: Pakistan is among the top five high-TB-burden countries; incidence ≈ 266 per 100,000 (approximate, WHO). Drug-resistant TB is a concern. GeneXpert MTB/RIF widely used.
- Typhoid: XDR typhoid outbreak in Hyderabad/Sindh from 2016; Pakistan was the first country to introduce typhoid conjugate vaccine into routine immunisation (2019). (Intestinal ulcers of typhoid are oval along Peyer's patches — differentiate from TB ulcers which are transverse/girdle.)
- Intestinal (abdominal) tuberculosis is common and mimics Crohn disease (ileocaecal region).
- Rheumatic fever / rheumatic heart disease remains common in Pakistan (mitral stenosis in young women, often presenting in pregnancy).
- Group A strep pyoderma (impetigo) in children in hot, humid, crowded settings → PSGN.
- Polio: Pakistan and Afghanistan remain the last countries with endemic wild poliovirus type 1 (relevant only if motor neuron/anterior horn disease is discussed).

## Haematology
- β-thalassaemia carrier rate in Pakistan ≈ 5–7% (commonly quoted); an estimated ≈ 5,000 or more children are born with thalassaemia major every year. Consanguineous (cousin) marriage is common, which raises the risk of autosomal recessive disease. Several provinces have passed thalassaemia prevention laws encouraging premarital/carrier screening; cascade screening of relatives of affected children is recommended.
- Anaemia is very common: National Nutrition Survey 2018 — about 54% of children under 5 and about 42% of women of reproductive age are anaemic; iron deficiency is the main cause. (Write "about half of young children and two-fifths of women".)
- Dietary vitamin B12 deficiency is seen in vegetarians; folate deficiency in pregnancy.

## Oncology
- Oral cancer: one of the most common cancers in Pakistan, especially in men (Karachi cancer registry ranks oral cavity cancer at or near the top in men). Risk factors: smokeless tobacco (naswar, gutka, mainpuri), betel quid/paan with areca (supari) nut, tobacco smoking, alcohol (less in Pakistan). Oral submucous fibrosis is linked to areca nut.
- Breast cancer: the commonest cancer in Pakistani women; Pakistan has one of the highest breast cancer rates in Asia; lifetime risk often quoted as about 1 in 9 women. Many women present late (stage III–IV) due to stigma and lack of screening. Pink Ribbon Pakistan runs awareness campaigns.
- Gallbladder carcinoma is relatively common in Pakistan and northern India (gallstone-associated; more in women).
- Hepatocellular carcinoma is mostly HCV-related in Pakistan.
- Oesophageal cancer: an "oesophageal cancer belt" includes parts of Balochistan/Afghanistan/Iran; SCC predominates; risk factors include very hot drinks (tea), naswar, smoking, nutritional deficiencies.
- Cervical cancer: lower reported incidence than in many countries, but most women are never screened. Pakistan began HPV vaccination of girls (9–14 years) in a national campaign in September 2025.
- Childhood cancers: leukaemia (ALL) is the commonest childhood cancer.

## Kidney / urology
- Pakistan lies in the Afro-Asian "stone belt": urolithiasis is very common (hot climate, dehydration, diet). Calcium oxalate stones are commonest; bladder stones in children still seen in rural areas.
- Diabetes and hypertension are the leading causes of chronic kidney disease in Pakistan.

## Endocrine / metabolic
- Diabetes: IDF Diabetes Atlas 2021 — about 26.7% of adults (≈ 33 million) in Pakistan, the third-largest number of people with diabetes in the world; national prevalence surveys (NDSP 2016–17) found ≈ 26% including undiagnosed. Write "about one in four adults".
- Iodine deficiency goitre was historically endemic in northern areas (Gilgit-Baltistan, KP, AJK); universal salt iodisation programme has improved coverage.
- Vitamin D deficiency is very common despite sunshine (covering, indoor life, diet) → osteomalacia/rickets still seen.

## Cardiovascular
- Ischaemic heart disease is a leading cause of death in Pakistan; South Asians develop coronary disease at a younger age.
- Hypertension affects about one in three adults (approximate).

## Lifestyle
- Tobacco (GATS Pakistan 2014): 19.1% of adults use tobacco in any form (men 31.8%, women 5.8%); smokeless use 7.7%. Relevant to oral, oesophageal, lung, bladder, pancreas cancers and COPD/atherosclerosis.
- Biomass fuel (wood, dung) cooking smoke in rural kitchens → COPD in non-smoking women.
- Silicosis among stone-crushing workers (e.g., in KP and Punjab) is a documented occupational hazard.
